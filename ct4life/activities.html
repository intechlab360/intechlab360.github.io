Cognitive Stimulation & Collaboration

Hands-On Learning Activities Unplugged Puzzles & Guided Digital Exploration

Our research investigates both unplugged (physical, conversational, hands-on) and plugged (visual interactive software) activities created specifically for older adults.

Category 01 Hands-On Physical

Unplugged Computational Thinking

No screens or computers! These activities use physical cards, everyday scenarios, and collaborative discussion to exercise algorithmic planning, pattern identification, and problem decomposition.

The Daily Route Optimizer

Concept: Algorithmic sequencing & optimization.
Participants work in pairs with route cards of Salem landmarks (Riverfront Park, Capitol Mall, Bush's Pasture) to design optimal travel recipes under everyday constraints like traffic or store hours.

25 mins • Small groups

Pattern Detective & Card Sorting

Concept: Pattern recognition & data categorization.
Using tactile sorting cards with varied traits, participants identify latent patterns, develop taxonomy rules, and experience how search algorithms organize large libraries of information.

20 mins • Logic puzzle

Recipe Decomposition Challenge

Concept: Problem abstraction & decomposition.
Participants take a complex culinary or woodworking project and break it down into atomic modular sub-routines, discovering how modular abstraction prevents overwhelm in problem solving.

30 mins • Collaborative
Category 02 Guided Digital Exploration

Plugged Visual Co-Design

Under the one-on-one guidance of friendly Willamette student mentors, participants try intuitive visual interfaces that demonstrate the joy of creative digital tools.

Visual Storyboarding & Block Puzzles

Tool: Block-based visual canvas.
Seniors pair with undergraduate student guides to snap together logic blocks that animate custom digital stories, family memories, or interactive greeting cards.

1-on-1 Student Guidance

iThinkSmart Logic Mini-Games

Tool: Accessible cognitive puzzles.
Enjoy gentle logic challenges like the River Crossing puzzle and Tower of Hanoi adapted for senior accessibility with large fonts, relaxed pacing, and clear goal visualization.

Relaxed & Self-Paced

Community Persona Co-Design

Tool: Interactive design thinking canvases.
Participants evaluate everyday apps (banking, healthcare, smart TVs) and redesign their interfaces to remove frustrating clutter, providing valuable data for our research lab.

Participatory Design
