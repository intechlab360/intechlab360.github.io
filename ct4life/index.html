Research Grounding

Rooted in Malcolm Knowles' Andragogy Principles

Lifelong learning is the self-motivated pursuit of growing knowledge for personal well-being and social connection. Unlike youth schooling (pedagogy), adult learning (andragogy) requires distinct, respectful principles:

  • 1
    Self-Concept: Moving from dependent instruction to self-directed, respectful exploration.
  • 2
    Life Experience: Treating past career and life knowledge as the richest asset in the room.
  • 3
    Readiness to Learn: Focusing on practical tasks relevant to everyday autonomy and interest.
  • 4
    Problem-Centered Orientation: Solving tangible puzzles rather than memorizing technical jargon.
  • 5
    Internal Motivation: Fostering curiosity, cognitive resilience, and joyful accomplishment.

What Is Computational Thinking (CT)?

Computational Thinking is not about becoming a computer programmer. It is a universal thought process we use every day to solve complex challenges:

Decomposition Breaking a big challenge down into small, bite-sized steps.
Pattern Recognition Spotting recurring trends and similarities in everyday life.
Abstraction Focusing on what truly matters and filtering out unnecessary clutter.
Algorithms Creating clear, step-by-step recipes or plans to accomplish goals.
