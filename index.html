About the Lab

Empowering Learners and Educators through Thoughtful Technology

At the iNTech (Interactive Technology for Learning) Research Lab, we investigate how emerging interactive computing systems—such as Generative AI, Virtual Reality (VR), and Smart Learning Environments (SLEs)—can be tailored to serve human agency rather than replace it.

Led by Dr. Fred Agbo in the School of Computing and Information Sciences (SCIS) at Willamette University, our interdisciplinary group designs, builds, and evaluates pedagogically grounded systems. We bridge computing education across the entire lifespan: from K-12 novices and undergraduate computer scientists to older adult lifelong learners.

Our Mission

To democratize computing education through inclusive, accessible, and scientifically grounded technology interventions.

Co-Design Pedagogy

Active participatory research engaging real teachers, students, and community seniors as equal design partners.

Core Expertise

Our Research Focus Domains

We conduct cutting-edge research across multiple domains to advance educational technology and human-computer interaction.

Smart Learning Environments

Designing intelligent, context-aware platforms that adapt to individual learning needs and provide real-time feedback using ML and learner modeling.

Computational Thinking & Andragogy

Developing unplugged and plugged approaches to teach problem-solving, abstraction, and logic across diverse populations, including adult and senior learners.

Inclusive Technology & HCC

Creating accessible and equitable educational interfaces ensuring computer science education reaches underrepresented groups and global south contexts.

AI in Education & GenAI

Investigating generative AI tools, prompt pedagogy, and teacher-in-the-loop scaffolding that prevents cognitive offloading and fosters deep comprehension.

HCI & Immersive Virtual Reality

Creating high-immersion VR simulations and gamified mini-games (iThinkSmart, Imikode) that turn abstract computational concepts into interactive 3D worlds.

Digital Pedagogy & Analytics

Mining student programming traces to detect misconception signals, evaluate formative competencies, and empower instructors with actionable dashboards.

2026 ACM SIGCSE / CS Education

From Correctness to Expert-Refined Misconception Signals with Human-in-the-Loop Validation in CS1 Programming Education

Aruleba, K.; Oyelere, S.S., Agbo, F.J., Jere, N. (2026). ACM Digital Library.

Knowledge tracing in introductory programming is commonly framed as predicting problem outcomes. This study demonstrates how expert-refined misconception signals derived from CodeWorkout trace data explain the true nature of student struggle in an actionable way.

View on ACM Digital Library
2025 CT4Life • Adult Learning

Exploring Andragogy Principles for Broadening Participation in Computing Education for Lifelong Learners

Agbo, F. J., & Everetts, C. (2025). ACM SIGCSE / Broadening Participation in Computing.

Examined how to design computing education for older adults (age 60+) using Malcolm Knowles' andragogy principles. Compares plugged vs. unplugged computational thinking interventions for social collaboration and cognitive stimulation.

ACM Link CT4Life Publications Hub
2025 Generative AI • ScienceDirect

Computing Education Using Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools: A Systematic Literature Review

Agbo, F. J., Olivia, C., Oguibe, G., Sanusi, I. T., & Sani, G. (2025). Computers and Education Open / ScienceDirect.

A comprehensive systematic literature review analyzing 78 empirical studies examining GenAI tools across CS curricula, identifying key opportunities, assessment challenges, and pedagogical strategies.

ScienceDirect Article
2023 HCI • Virtual Reality • Springer

Examining the Relationships Between Students’ Perceptions of Technology, Pedagogy, and Cognition in Immersive VR Mini Games

Agbo, F. J., Olaleye, S. A., Bower, M., & Oyelere, S. S. (2023). Smart Learning Environments, Springer.

Investigated how game element clarity, immersion, and interaction in the iThinkSmart VR platform predict learners' reflective thinking and comprehension in computational thinking.
